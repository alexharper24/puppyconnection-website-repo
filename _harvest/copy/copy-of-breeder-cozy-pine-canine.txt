Edward and Malinda
Edward and Malinda Ramer have been raising 10–12 lb Miniature Dachshunds for over twelve years, focusing exclusively on this beloved breed. They live on a three-acre farmette with their five children—ranging in age from two to eleven—where puppies are raised right in the middle of busy family life.
Caring for the dogs is a shared effort. Dad handles morning chores, while Mom and the kids take care of kennel duties each evening. The children play a special role in daily puppy care, from cuddling and gentle handling to helping with early training. Their setup includes a six-pen kennel with outdoor runs and a dedicated play yard, giving puppies space to explore while remaining safely supervised.
The Ramers’ philosophy is simple and heartfelt: raise healthy, friendly, well-socialized puppies and make sure families are happy with their new companions. By specializing in only one breed and raising their females in-house for several generations, they are able to stay closely connected to their dogs’ temperaments, structure, and overall consistency.
Puppy socialization begins early. The children start holding puppies around two weeks of age and play with them every evening as they grow. When weather allows, puppies are taken outside to experience grass and fresh air; on colder or rainy days, they’re brought into the house for indoor playtime. One way this program really stands out is the introduction of collars and leashes starting at eight weeks of age, helping puppies become comfortable with basic handling and early training before they ever leave home.
Puppies are born in a kennel with heated tile flooring and remain with their mother throughout the early weeks. Around four to five weeks, they begin eating puppy food. Vaccinations are administered at five, seven, and twelve weeks of age, with deworming every two weeks beginning at three weeks. All puppies are microchipped. Veterinary care is provided by Dr. Weldy’s Associates.
Each puppy goes home with a complete vaccination and deworming record, a health certificate when required, microchip documentation, and starter food with feeding instructions.
If a Miniature Dachshund raised in a family setting—complete with early leash exposure and lots of kid love—sounds like the right fit, the Ramers are always glad to share more about their dogs and daily routine.
We reserve Sundays for church and family, so we are not available for phone calls or visits on that day.
