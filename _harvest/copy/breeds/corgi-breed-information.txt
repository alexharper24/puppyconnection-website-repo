Pembroke Welsh Corgi Breed Information
The Pembroke Welsh Corgi: A Royal Companion with a Big Personality
Short in stature but huge in personality, the Pembroke Welsh Corgi is one of the most beloved breeds in the world—and for good reason. Famous for their fox-like faces, perky ears, and wiggly little tails (or lack thereof!), these sturdy herders are full of spunk, smarts, and heart. Known for being Queen Elizabeth II’s favorite breed, the Pembroke Corgi has earned its royal reputation both on palace grounds and in family homes everywhere.
A Distinctive and Darling Appearance
Pembroke Welsh Corgis are instantly recognizable with their long bodies, short legs, and bright, intelligent expressions. Weighing between 25 and 30 pounds and standing about 10 to 12 inches tall, they’re a compact breed that’s surprisingly athletic and agile.
Their thick double coats come in colors like red, sable, fawn, and tri-color, often paired with white markings. While they do shed (especially during seasonal changes), regular brushing helps keep their coats healthy and manageable. And yes, those signature butts are just as adorable in real life as they are on the internet!
Clever, Confident, and Full of Character
Corgis were originally bred to herd cattle, and that working-dog intelligence and drive are still alive and well in the breed today. They’re quick learners who love to be challenged, and they often excel in obedience, agility, and even tricks.
Beyond their brains, Pembrokes are famously affectionate and loyal. They adore being part of the family and have a strong desire to be involved in whatever their people are doing. Whether it's tagging along on errands or keeping you company while you relax, Corgis are always close by, ready for action—or a belly rub.
Family-Friendly and Full of Fun
Pembroke Welsh Corgis make fantastic family pets. They tend to be great with children, playful without being overwhelming, and social with other dogs and even cats (with the right introductions). They’re naturally watchful and protective, making them excellent watchdogs who are quick to alert you—but not typically excessive barkers when properly trained.
They’re just as happy in a suburban backyard as they are in a city apartment, as long as they get enough exercise and companionship. A bored Corgi can be a mischievous one, so daily play, walks, and mental engagement are key.
A Small Dog with Big Energy
Corgis may be low to the ground, but they have plenty of energy to spare. Their herding instincts mean they’re always on alert, and they love to run, play, and explore. Puzzle toys, training games, and even canine sports like herding trials or agility can be great outlets for their energy.
That said, they also know how to settle down and relax once they’ve had their fill of activity—often curling up at your feet with a happy sigh.

Top 5 Frequently Asked Questions About Pembroke Welsh Corgis

Are Pembroke Welsh Corgis good family dogs?
Yes! Corgis are affectionate, intelligent, and full of character, which makes them a wonderful addition to many families. They’re especially known for their loyalty and love of being involved in every part of family life—from playtime to snuggle time. Corgis usually do well with children and can get along with other pets, though their herding instincts might make them try to “herd” everyone at times (nipping at heels is a common puppy habit that training helps with). With early socialization and clear boundaries, they become delightful, devoted companions.
Do Pembroke Welsh Corgis require a lot of grooming?
They do shed—a lot! Corgis have a thick double coat that sheds year-round and more heavily during seasonal changes (spring and fall). Weekly brushing (or even daily during heavy shedding periods) will help keep their coat healthy and reduce the amount of hair on your clothes and furniture. While they don’t need professional grooming often, regular brushing, nail trimming, ear checks, and dental care are essential parts of keeping them looking and feeling their best.
Are P embroke Welsh Corgis easy to train?
Definitely! Corgis are smart, eager to learn, and enjoy having a job to do. They respond very well to positive reinforcement training—treats, toys, and praise all work wonders. Because they’re bright and energetic, they thrive when mentally stimulated. However, they can be a bit stubborn or bossy if they think they’re in charge, so early, consistent training helps establish good manners. Corgis are fantastic at learning tricks, agility, obedience, and even herding tasks if you give them the opportunity.
Do P embroke Welsh Corgis have health issues?
Like all breeds, Corgis can be prone to certain health conditions. The most common concerns include hip dysplasia, degenerative myelopathy, progressive retinal atrophy (PRA), and intervertebral disc disease (IVDD) due to their long backs. They also tend to gain weight easily, so keeping them at a healthy weight is important to avoid added strain on their spine and joints. Regular vet checkups, a balanced diet, and health testing in breeding stock help support their long-term well-being.
How much exercise do P embroke Welsh Corgis need?
Corgis are active little dogs with a surprising amount of energy! They were bred to herd livestock, so they do best with at least 45 minutes to an hour of daily exercise. This can include brisk walks, games of fetch, puzzle toys, or even dog sports like agility and rally. Corgis love being mentally challenged just as much as they enjoy physical play. While they can adapt to various living situations, they need regular activity to stay happy and avoid boredom-based mischief (they can be quite the clever troublemakers when under-stimulated!).
