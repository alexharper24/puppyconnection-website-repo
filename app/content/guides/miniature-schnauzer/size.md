A grown Miniature Schnauzer weighs 11 to 20 pounds, according to AKC, and stands 12 to 14 inches at the shoulder under the breed standard. {{parent_weights}}

The standard itself sets only height, so the 11 to 20 pound range is AKC's guide rather than a rule, and a puppy's own parents are the better clue to where it will land.

## What does the breed standard say about size?

The AKC Miniature Schnauzer standard gives a size of 12 to 14 inches and disqualifies a dog under 12 inches or over 14. It describes a sturdily built dog, nearly square in body length to height, with plenty of bone and no suggestion of toyishness.

Because the standard gives no weight, a Schnauzer can sit above or below AKC's range and still be the right height for the breed.

## Is there a toy or teacup Miniature Schnauzer?

Not in the standard. The 12 inch minimum is a disqualification line, so a Miniature Schnauzer under 12 inches falls outside the breed standard, and a size word like toy or teacup is the breeder's own description. Ask how tall the parents stand as adults if you see one.

## How can you estimate a Schnauzer puppy's adult weight?

Ask how tall each parent stands, how much each weighs, and whether either is still young. A puppy from a pair near 12 inches and the low end of AKC's weights is likely to finish smaller than one from a pair near 14 inches. If the same pair has had a litter before, ask what those puppies weigh as adults now, since that is the nearest guide you can get.

Judging by eye is harder with this coat, since the standard describes a double coat with a hard, wiry outer coat, a close undercoat and fairly thick furnishings, so the parents' measurements are more reliable than a photo.

## When does a Miniature Schnauzer stop growing?

Small breeds such as this one are adult sized by six months to a year in VCA Animal Hospitals' guidance. Expect a Miniature Schnauzer to be close to its adult height within its first year, and ask your vet to track its weight and body condition from there.

## What health checks matter for a Miniature Schnauzer?

Liver shunts, urinary stones, pancreatitis, cataracts and hyperlipidemia are on AKC's list for the breed, and it asks owners to keep an eye on the dog's overall condition and bring questions to their vet. Vets can score that condition on a nine point chart from the World Small Animal Veterinary Association, where a dog at the ideal score has a clear waist from above and an abdominal tuck from the side.

Parents used for breeding should have had the heart exam and the ophthalmologist's eye exam the national breed club recommends. Ask to see both sets of results.
