A grown male Cocker Spaniel stands about 14.5 to 15.5 inches at the shoulder and weighs 25 to 30 pounds, and a female stands about 13.5 to 14.5 inches and weighs 20 to 25 pounds, according to AKC. {{parent_weights}}

The breed standard is precise about height and silent on weight, so it is the place to start.

## What does the breed standard say about height?

The AKC Cocker Spaniel standard sets an ideal height of 15 inches for an adult male and 14 inches for an adult female, and allows half an inch above or below. A male over 15½ inches or a female over 14½ inches is disqualified. A male under 14½ inches or a female under 13½ inches is penalized but not disqualified. The standard measures height from the ground to the top of the shoulder blades with the dog standing naturally.

The 20 to 30 pound figures above come from AKC's breed profile.

## How is it different in size from an English Cocker?

AKC treats the English Cocker Spaniel as a separate breed, and it is the larger of the two. AKC lists English Cocker males at 16 to 17 inches and 28 to 34 pounds and females at 15 to 16 inches and 26 to 32 pounds. If a listing or a breeder uses the word Cocker loosely, ask which breed the parents are, because the size you can expect follows from that answer.

## How can you estimate a Cocker puppy's adult size?

Ask the breeder for both parents' adult heights and weights and whether either is still maturing. A puppy's frame comes from those two dogs, so parents near the middle of the standard point to a puppy that finishes near the middle as well. If the same pair has had a litter before, ask what those puppies grew to.

Males and females differ by about an inch in height and five pounds in weight in AKC's figures, so expect a male puppy to finish a little larger than his sisters.

## When does a Cocker Spaniel stop growing?

VCA Animal Hospitals says medium-sized dogs usually reach their adult size at around a year, and small-breed dogs between 6 and 12 months. At 20 to 30 pounds, a Cocker Spaniel sits between those groups, so expect most of its growth to be done within its first year.

Grooming carries on after growth ends, and AKC notes that thorough grooming of the Cocker coat helps prevent mats, and that the ears need regular cleaning, especially after a bath, to avoid infections.

## What health checks and weight matter for a Cocker?

AKC says the national parent club, the American Spaniel Club, recommends health testing for breeding dogs, and that a conscientious breeder can show records of the parents' testing for hips, patellas and eyes. AKC gives the breed a life expectancy of 10 to 14 years. Keeping a Cocker at a healthy weight also matters, and in a study of middle-aged, neutered dogs at North American veterinary hospitals, Cocker Spaniels in normal body condition lived to a median of about 14.9 years, against about 13.4 for overweight Cockers.
